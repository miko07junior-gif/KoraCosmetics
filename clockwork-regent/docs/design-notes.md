# Clockwork Regent: design notes (gray-box stage)

Every check here runs against a vanilla Minecraft 1.21.x stand-in for the player,
not Kora. Kora's own poses, scale and cape handling may change any of these results.

Numbers are in model pixels (px = 1/16 block) unless marked as blocks. Blocks include
vanilla's 15/16 player scale. Everything is regenerated by the scripts in `../tools`;
if a number here disagrees with a script's output, the script wins.

## 1. Concept to geometry

| Brief | Gray box |
|---|---|
| Folded: leading edges rise into a pointed arch, framing the head without covering it | Each leading edge has three segments, like a bird's wing: humerus straight up (7), forearm leaning in 20 deg (6), hand leaning in 52 deg (8.7). The hands meet at an apex 8.5 px above the head (0.5 blocks); with the ivory finials the top is 10.2 px above (0.6 blocks). The closed feather tiers hang beside the wearer as the niche's side walls. |
| Spread: art deco sunburst, three stepped tiers (coverts, secondaries, primaries) | Per wing: 5 coverts (8 px), 6 secondaries (13 px), 7 primaries (21 px), radiating from the shoulder hub between 74 and 152 deg (rising). Each blade is a blackened-bronze plate with a brass rib (ivory on the coverts), so the rays read as brass lines on dark bronze. |
| Unfold = statue waking; light out first, then each tier in turn | The arch splits at the apex, the leading edges tilt out, then coverts, secondaries and primaries rise in turn (key poses `arch_split`, `coverts_open`, `secondaries_open`, `spread` in `../design/regent_graybox.json`). |
| One light source: escapement heart at the root of the spine | Heart (4 x 5 x 3.5) on the small of the back, with escape wheel and pallet bones. The spine runs straight back from it, then up to the yoke; amber seams run along every blade. |
| Hierarchy spine -> joint -> feather group, named pivots | `regent > spine > shoulder_{r,l} > {humerus > forearm > hand, coverts/secondaries/primaries > blades, gears, pistons}`; 67 bones, 148 cubes |

## 2. The clearance envelope, and why the frame stands 11 px off the back

The vanilla pose envelope (`../tools/player.py`) covers:
- head pitch -90 to +90 and yaw +-75;
- walking and sprinting, with the full limb swing;
- sneak and sneak-walk, with the body pitched 0.5 rad;
- the attack swing;
- bow, crossbow, shield block, item use, spyglass and goat horn;
- armor (helmet and chestplate dilate 1.0, leggings 0.5, boots 1.0 on the lower 5 px);
- classic and slim arms.

Two things fill almost all the space behind a player:
- A sprinting arm in armor swings back 1 rad and reaches z = 10.9 behind the shoulder at y = 18.6.
- A helmet looking up swings its top-back corner up to about 11.4 behind the neck (y 19-35, |x| <= 7).

Together they leave no clean path from the back to the wings closer than about 11 px. Measured:

| Frame standoff (front face, z) | Result |
|---|---|
| 12 | clean |
| 11.5 | clean, closest contact 0.25 px or more (chosen) |
| 11 | clean at 16 sprint phases; at 64 phases the closed primaries clip a sprinting pauldron by 0.02 px |
| 10 | primaries clip a sprinting pauldron by 0.88 px |
| 9 | 1.27 px |

The heart has a narrow window: it has to sit between the sprinting legging tops and a
sneaking, looked-up helmet.

| Heart (w x h x d, y range) | Worst contact |
|---|---|
| 5 x 5.5 x 3.5, y 13.5-19 | 0.54 px into the attack-swing pauldron; 0.47 px into sprinting leggings |
| 4 x 5 x 3.5, y 14.5-19.5 | clean (chosen) |
| 4 x 5.5 x 3, y 15-20.5 | 4.5 px into a sneaking, looked-up helmet |

Vanilla's own head sinks into its body when looking up: about 1 px at 30 deg, 2 px at
90 deg, 4.4 px when sneaking. A head contact no deeper than that in the same pose is
counted as "no worse than vanilla" and not as a clip (`player.vanilla_self_clip_depth`).

Full results are in `../renders/checks/clip_*.txt` and `.json`; section 7 has the summary.

## 3. Capes: a structural conflict

The vanilla cape is 10 px wide (|x| <= 5), lies flush on the back from the shoulder
line down, and swings back up to about 113 deg (sprinting, falling). Any back-mounted
part at |x| <= 5 between the shoulders and the knees, within 17 px of the cape hinge,
is inside that sweep. That is where the brief puts the bronze spine and the heart.

The body is otherwise boxed in: the cape covers the back, the arms cover the sides,
and the head covers the top. So no mount on the torso can avoid a swinging vanilla
cape. Which way to go depends on Kora (intake question 6):
- **Kora hides the cape when wings are worn, or draws only one:** no conflict. This is the assumption so far.
- **Kora re-anchors the cape behind the wings:** re-run `clipcheck.py --cape` against Kora's cape position.
- **Both are drawn with vanilla physics:** "no clipping against a worn cape" can't be met by any back-mounted mechanical wing. The fallback is to move the heart and spine out of the cape's sweep, which drops the spine-on-the-back idea. That's a brief-level decision for the owner, not a tuning change.

## 4. Spread layout: three candidates, one kept

| Layout | Fan (primaries) | At 30 blocks | Kept? |
|---|---|---|---|
| lowered | 22-106 deg, radiating out and down | reads as bat or moth wings | no |
| rising, narrow | 78-146 deg, 19 px | fans merge into solid triangles: "horns" or "ears" | no |
| rising, wide | 74-152 deg, 21 px primaries | rays separate (about 2 px tip gaps), reads as a radiating crown or sunburst behind the wearer | **yes** |

The rising fan matches the Chrysler crown, which radiates upward, and the heraldic royal
posture ("eagle displayed, wings elevated"). The unfold also echoes the Peacock Clock's
tail lifting. All three layouts are kept in `rig.SPREADS` for comparison renders.

## 6. Value key and F5

**Measured value shares** of the visible cosmetic (bronze / brass / ivory+amber), with the
wearer in place. The target is 60 / 30 / 10.

| | Folded | Spread |
|---|---|---|
| back | 62 / 26 / 12 | 58 / 29 / 13 |
| 3/4 back | 71 / 21 / 8 | 64 / 25 / 11 |
| front | 66 / 34 / 1 | 65 / 34 / 1 |

The front views carry almost no ivory, because the ivory ribs sit on the back layer. Ivory
enamel on the front faces of the hub cases and the coverts should fix that at the painting
stage.

**F5 occlusion** at 1080p FOV 70, looking level: the share of the screen the cosmetic
covers beyond the player model (`../tools/f5check.py`).

| State | Screen | Centre third | Crosshair box |
|---|---|---|---|
| folded | 3.4% | 21% | 0% |
| spread (rising) | 7.4% | 47% | 0% |

The crosshair is never covered at FOV 70. Spread covers nearly half the centre third. The
brief's "no blocking the F5 view" needs a yardstick, and the obvious one is Kora's
existing wings measured the same way (intake section 4). If spread exceeds their range,
the options are:
- spread only on menu/emote triggers;
- a smaller fan (primaries 21 -> 17 px cuts the span to about 3.1 blocks);
- asking Kora whether it fades the wearer's own cosmetics in F5.

## 7. Clipping summary

Wing states checked:
- folded;
- unfold at 25 / 50 / 75%;
- spread;
- the three staggered tier keys;
- the rejected lowered spread.

Run with `clipcheck.py`; results are in `../renders/checks/`.

| Run | Poses | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Common (look every 15 deg, sprint 32 phases x 3 idle-sway ages, sneak, attack, item poses; classic arms, full armor) | 718 | **0 clips** in all 9 states |
| Rare (adds the overhead trident throw), look every 30 deg | 532 | **0 clips** |
| Slim arms, no armor, look every 30 deg | 516 | **0 clips** |
| Vanilla cape worn (folded + spread; motion poses, level look, 12 cape angles) | - | pending: run in progress |

The closest contacts that aren't vanilla-excused:
- closed primaries vs a sprinting pauldron, 0.25 px or more since the 11.5 standoff;
- the heart vs the chestplate, 0.25 px by design;
- the heart vs the attack-swing pauldron, 0.14 px.

Pistons stay inside their travel in every layout:
- humerus pistons: L 6.73-7.33 within 6.0-9.0;
- primaries pistons: L 6.61-9.49 within 6.0-10.0.

The SAT gap is a lower bound on true separation, so these clearances are conservative.
Pose sampling is discrete, so a contact thinner than the sampling step can still hide
between samples. That is why the standoff went from 11 to 11.5 after a finer sprint
sweep turned up a 0.02 px clip.

## 8. Open risks for the step 2 gates

- **PvP head reading.** The arch apex is 8.5 px (0.5 block) above the head, and the finials 10.2 px. At 30 blocks on 1080p FOV 70 there's a 2-4 px ring of air between head and arch, so the head stays a separate shape in every silhouette render. Whether PvP players agree is the gate. If it fails, lower the arch: lower `hand.fold` / `forearm.fold`, or shorten the humerus.
- **Standoff.** The 0.84-block depth is forced by the clearance envelope. From the side it reads as a frame behind the wearer, a niche the statue stands in front of. If Kora's poses are narrower than vanilla's (or its arms don't swing as far), the standoff can come in; re-run the sweep.
- **Bone count** (67) against Kora's limit; blades can be merged per tier if needed.
- **Thin parts at FOV 110:** the hands are 3 px wide, which is about 1.5 screen px at 30 blocks on 1080p FOV 110.
